Policy PDF encodes (your answers should match this):
  • Match keys: PO Number + PO Line Item ID
  • Tolerance: lesser of 2% of PO line amount or USD 50 (Math node, NOT Matcher)
  • Unreceived: auto-approve if under $500 AND Tier-1; else buyer review
  • Output: multi-tab Excel — Summary, Matched, Exceptions (no Slack in v1)

--- Step 7: LLM interview (natural chat → pipeline design) ---

WHY THIS PHASE EXISTS
  Nexus asks like a colleague, not a form. Answer in plain business language.
  Behind the scenes it extracts match keys, formulas, thresholds, and routing rules
  to wire Matcher → Math → Decision → Output on the canvas.

  Tip: You don't need to say "Math node" or "MIN formula" — describe the business
  rule in your own words. Nexus should still capture the technical spec.

  Nexus may ask 5–15 questions. Below are typical prompts with human-friendly replies.
  Each reply includes what the pipeline should pick up — use this to verify the canvas.

--- Q1: How do you tie a PO line to a receipt and an invoice? ---

NEXUS (example):
  "When you're matching these three files, what fields actually line up a PO line
   with its receipt and invoice?"

USER (paste this):
  We always tie things together on the PO number and the line item ID — that's how
  buyers, receiving, and AP all reference the same line.

→ PIPELINE: Matcher (structural) keys = po_number + po_line_id

--- Q2: Vendor names don't always match exactly ---

NEXUS (example):
  "I noticed vendor names can differ — like 'Dell Computers' on a receipt vs
   'Dell Inc' on the invoice. Should I still treat those as the same vendor?"

USER:
  Yes, please. Names are messy across systems — don't fail a good match just because
  the spelling is slightly different.

→ PIPELINE: Matcher enables semantic entity normalization on vendor_name

--- Q3: How much dollar difference is OK before you care? ---

NEXUS (example):
  "What size gap between the PO amount and what was invoiced is acceptable?"

USER:
  Small stuff is fine. Our policy is whichever is smaller — two percent of the PO line
  or fifty dollars. A couple of cents of rounding shouldn't create noise, but anything
  bigger than that should get flagged.

→ PIPELINE: Math gate formula = MIN(0.02 × po_line_amount, 50.00)
              (Matcher records the match; Math applies the tolerance)

--- Q4: What if goods were never received? ---

NEXUS (example):
  "Some invoices might not have a matching goods receipt yet. How should those go?"

USER:
  It depends. If it's under five hundred dollars and the vendor is Tier-1, AP can
  auto-approve. Everything else — higher dollar amount or not a Tier-1 vendor —
  send to the buyer to review.

→ PIPELINE: Decision agent on Matcher exceptions port
              Rule: IF invoice_total < 500 AND vendor_tier = Tier-1 → auto-approve
                    ELSE → buyer review
              (Knowledge node supplies tier rules from policy PDF)

--- Q5: What do you want at the end? ---

NEXUS (example):
  "How should I package the results for your team?"

USER:
  An Excel workbook works best — Summary tab, Matched tab, and Exceptions tab.
  I'll download it from here; we don't need Slack or email alerts.

→ PIPELINE: Output node → multi-tab Excel (Summary | Matched | Exceptions)

--- FOLLOW-UPS (if Nexus asks) ---

[Unmatched or over-tolerance rows]
USER:
  Anything we couldn't match, or that's past our tolerance, should land on Exceptions
  for the buyer to work through.

→ PIPELINE: Residual / exception routing → Exceptions tab

[Quantity vs price]
USER:
  If they invoiced more than we received, flag the quantity mismatch. And if the
  dollar gap is bigger than our two-percent-or-fifty rule, flag that too.

→ PIPELINE: Qty variance check + Math tolerance gate (separate paths to Exceptions)

[Ready to confirm]
